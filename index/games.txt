=== Web Games ===

Landbridge — Two countries, far apart on the map, and a single rule that turns the world into a puzzle: get from the start to the destination by naming countries that each share a land border with the last one, one hop at a time, walking overland the whole way. Name a country that touches your last one and it joins your chain; name one that doesn't and you lose a life. The game already knows the shortest route by walking the real border-adjacency graph of the world, so it grades every crossing you make — perfect if you match its route, a little longer if you wander — and reveals the optimal path at the end so you can see what you missed. It's a pure test of your mental map: do you know that Turkey touches Georgia, that Panama touches Colombia, that Portugal only touches Spain? The whole world — every border and the graph of which country touches which — is bundled in the page from public-domain map data, so there are no map tiles, no location services and no tracking, and it works offline. Three modes with real spread: Trek (a medium crossing of a few hops, great for a quick round), Odyssey (a long haul across a whole landmass, six to eleven hops at best, plan the corridor not just the next step), and Chain (no destination at all — just build the longest border-chain you can, never repeating a country, before you get stuck). You name countries by typing, with undo always free, so the only real cost is not knowing a border. Play a fresh random pair, the daily pair the whole world shares, or race two to six friends peer-to-peer on the identical route — join by scanning a QR, opening a link, or typing the room code, and the results screen shows everyone's route and the shortest one. Nothing about the route ever crosses the wire; every device works it out from the same seed. [puzzle, geography, versus] https://landbridge.benrichardson.dev

Beeline — Somewhere on Earth, one country is hidden, and you find it with nothing but distance and direction. Type any country to guess it, and each guess turns into three clues: how far it is from the mystery as the crow flies, an arrow pointing the way, and how warm you're getting — closer countries glow hotter on the map. Name the hidden country in as few guesses as you can. The whole world is bundled in the page from public-domain map data, so there are no map tiles, no photos and no tracking, and it works offline. Three modes with real spread: Silhouette (the mystery's shape is shown from the start, a shape-and-geography puzzle), Blind (no outline, just your mental map of the world), and Territories (no outline, and the net widens to lesser-known states, dependencies and disputed territories for the map obsessives). Play a fresh random country for an endless streak, or the daily country that everyone in the world shares, or race two to six friends peer-to-peer on the same hidden country — join by scanning a QR, opening a link, or typing the room code, and the results screen shows everyone's route and the answer. Nothing about the country ever crosses the wire; every device works it out from the same seed. [puzzle, geography, versus] https://beeline.benrichardson.dev

Amble — An unhurried journey down one long road, and a single rule that makes it a game: after you move, the turn passes to whoever is now furthest behind. So you can travel as far along the road as you like, but every stop you race past is a turn you hand your rivals — a short hop keeps you at the back and hands you another turn soon, while a long leap grabs a coveted distant spot before anyone else can, and then leaves you idle while everyone behind mops up everything you skipped. Stops are single-occupancy on a one-way road, so being there when a rival passes denies it to them for good. No dice, no combat, no hidden cards — just the quiet question of how much you give up to reach one spot first. Every traveller may claim the same number of stops before the road ends, so the whole game is which stops to spend them on: commit to one panorama deep, collect a spread of souvenirs, soak in springs for the majority, or rush to eat the best meal at each first-come inn. Three roads that play differently — Towpath (short and spring-heavy, the end races decide it), Long Road (the full journey, deep views and inn timing), and Wayward (the views scattered every seed, a routing puzzle that never learns twice). Play solo against travellers of the road, or with two to five friends peer-to-peer over a shared link, scan, or code. [board, versus] https://amble.benrichardson.dev

Kilter — Stack bricks that really fall. There is no grid: every brick is a rigid body with real weight and friction, so nothing snaps into place and nothing ever locks. You slide a brick to aim it, tap to spin it, and drop — and it tumbles down under gravity and settles wherever the pile and its own momentum leave it, level or leaning or sliding clean off the edge. The tower is actually balanced, not notionally balanced, and you lose height by toppling, not by filling a row. That one change is the whole game: the safe wide spot, or the risky reach that gains real height and might send your top three bricks sliding off. Three modes with different nerve: Ascent (unlimited bricks, race a friend to a height line, a topple just costs you time), Salvage (twelve bricks only, and every one that slides off is gone for good), and Spire (a plinth barely a brick wide, where a wide base topples at once and balance is everything). Drag to aim, tap to rotate, no D-pad. Play solo, chase the daily tower, or race up to six friends peer-to-peer — each building their own tower from the same brick order, with a Gust you earn to blow a crosswind at whoever's tower is highest. [arcade, versus, physics] https://kilter.benrichardson.dev

Uproar — Pass the phone round the group and get louder than the last person. On your turn the phone counts you in and listens, and you have to beat the bar — clear it and the bar climbs to meet you and passes on; miss it and you're out. Last one standing, the one willing to be loudest in a public place, wins. The input is your actual voice volume, measured by the phone as a single number: nothing you say is recorded, understood, or sent anywhere, and the reason you lose is that you bottle it in front of your friends. Three modes are three different skills — Bellow (just be loud), Sustain (hold your voice above a line for longer each turn), and Pinpoint (land inside a band that rises and shrinks). Solo, it's a personal ladder: how high can you climb before your voice gives out. No microphone, or you'd rather not use it? You get the tap version automatically — same game, your thumb instead of your voice. One phone, no login, no network. [party, voice] https://uproar.benrichardson.dev

Ballast — Stack the falling pieces and clear full rows. That part you know in five seconds. Then there is the rule the whole game is built on: the well hangs from a pivot. Every block in your stack has weight, so a lopsided stack makes the whole board lean, and you can watch it tip. A leaning well curves the fall — pieces slide downhill as they drop, so the far side goes genuinely out of reach, which is exactly where you need to build to straighten up. The ghost always shows where a piece will really land, drift and all, so it is a puzzle rather than a betrayal. Clear a row while the well is level and it scores double. Watch for the ingot: a single very heavy block that fills any hole and wrecks your balance doing it. Three wells that are three different problems: Trim (balanced), Careen (narrow and twitchy, always listing) and Broadside (wide and slow, and you start loaded with cargo). Play solo for a score, race three bots, take the daily well everyone gets, or play peer-to-peer with up to five friends — where clearing rows sends ballast to whoever is ahead, landing on their heavy side so it both raises their stack and worsens their lean. [puzzle, versus] https://ballast.benrichardson.dev

Unstrung — A word game about making the board rather than reading it. You get one long strand of letters; find a run of touching letters that spells a word and cut it out, and fresh thread is spliced straight into the gap. The two joins it leaves are seams, they are permanent and visible, and a word that spans one scores TRIPLE. So every cut is a choice about what the strand can spell next — and the seam you leave behind is the one your opponent gets to use, which makes taking the small word instead of the big one a real decision from the first move. The free play is always there (the fresh thread is a short word) but it is the cheap one; the points are in the bridges you build. Everyone gets the same number of cuts, and the results screen shows the best line a solver could find on your exact strand. Uses a curated list of everyday words, not a tournament dictionary, so obscure three-letter words do not count. Three modes: Strand, Deepcut (nothing under four letters), and Twinfold (two strands at once). Solo, against the machine, a daily strand, or peer-to-peer for two to four. [word, versus] https://unstrung.benrichardson.dev

Undercut — Race strategy with no driving at all. The race runs itself forward and your only move is the pit call: box now, and onto which tyre. Every place you gain or lose comes down to a decision — the undercut (pit early onto fresh rubber and leapfrog a rival stuck on old tyres) versus the overcut (stay out on a dying set and hope the rain comes). Read the track temperature, which decides the fastest compound, and in the wet-capable races a rain forecast that is a probability, not a fact, firming as the window nears — so a 40% forecast really does rain about 40% of the time, and betting early is a genuine gamble. Push a set past its cliff and it falls off a shelf; the wrong tyre for the conditions is a disaster. Three modes: a dry Sprint, a coin-flip Grand Prix, and a volatile Deluge. Solo against the field, an identical-seed share, or peer-to-peer for up to six ranked by time. [strategy, versus] https://undercut.benrichardson.dev

Boxbox —A pit-stop game where the call is everything and the tapping is only how you carry it out. The car comes in with partial telemetry — tyres, fuel, front wing, brakes — and a driver on the radio who is subjective and sometimes plain wrong, and you choose which jobs the crew does before it arrives. Every job you add costs seconds in the stop; every fault you leave costs seconds on track; and a system you send out in the red blows up. A full service is never right and neither is the bare minimum — you triage. Then you run the stop itself, jacking up before the wheels come off and hitting GO only when it's all clear. Lowest total time wins, and the results show the perfect line on your exact seed. Solo against a field, or peer-to-peer for up to six on the identical race. [strategy, versus] https://boxbox.benrichardson.dev

Rootbound — There is no board, only pieces, and they all touch. Every piece from both players forms one single colony that you may never split in two, and you win by filling all six cells around your rival's Heartroot — with pieces of either colour, so walling your own Heartroot in is how you lose. The twist is polarity: a piece surrounded by your own colour is safe and shuffles one cell, while a piece outnumbered by the enemy is exposed and moves its full range. Range is not a property of a piece, it is a property of where it stands, so retreating is slow and overextending is fast. Solo against a three-strength engine, or peer-to-peer with a friend. [board, versus] https://rootbound.benrichardson.dev

Sporeline — Open-field maze defence with no fixed path: the towers you drop ARE the walls, so every placement is about folding the creeps' road back on itself to buy your guns more seconds. You can never fully seal the Gate — that placement is simply rejected — so you are always shaping a corridor rather than closing one, and the game previews the road you would create before you commit. Kills pay gold you split between a tighter maze and sending a spore into a rival's field, which drops a creep on them and raises your income for the rest of the match. Last Heart beating wins. Solo against bots, or peer-to-peer for up to four. [strategy, versus] https://sporeline.benrichardson.dev

Lastlight — One generator is all that stands between your settlement and the freeze, and only what sits inside its ring of warmth actually works. Homes shelter nobody outside it, gathering huts must sit on the very tile they harvest, and workshops boost only the neighbours they touch — so every placement is a puzzle against a closing frost. Burn coal to push the ring back out, but a bigger ring costs more to hold. Solo against the cold, or race friends on the identical map. [strategy, versus] https://lastlight.benrichardson.dev

Turntide — Draughts with one rule replaced: a jump never kills. Leap an adjacent enemy and it flips to your colour and stays exactly where it is, so all 24 stones remain on the board and every capture swings two at once. Material circulates instead of accumulating, which means an early lead is not a won game. Kings can't be farmed (a flipped king loses its crown) and controlling 18 of the 24 stones wins instantly. Solo against a three-strength engine, or peer-to-peer with a friend. [board, versus] https://turntide.benrichardson.dev

Changeling — Real chess, all the normal rules, win by checkmate — with one rule bolted on: the instant your piece captures, it turns into whatever it just captured. Take a pawn with your queen and your queen becomes a pawn; ram a pawn into their queen and that pawn is a queen. Only the king is immune. Solo against an engine, or peer-to-peer with a friend. [board, versus] https://changeling.benrichardson.dev

Frostward — A creeping frost spreads only into the wind's forward arc, so it grows as a cone that sweeps the board like a searchlight. Everyone secretly plays one card a turn and every Veer adds up, which means the only way to get the cone off your own hearths is to point it at somebody else's. Last hearth still lit wins. [board, versus] https://frostward.benrichardson.dev

Deepwatch — A co-op game of nerve played in total silence: surface a hand of deep-sea creatures onto one line, shallowest first, with no numbers anywhere and no chat. A card's height on the depth gauge is its depth, and the only thing you know about anyone else is how long they've been holding. [card, co-op] https://deepwatch.benrichardson.dev

Emberwake — Outrun a rising wall of dark. Your momentum is your speed, your life and your score all at once, so clipping a hazard doesn't kill you — it slows you, and the dark closes. Thread hazards for a refill, and spend it on a surge to clear the chasms. [arcade, daily] https://emberwake.benrichardson.dev
