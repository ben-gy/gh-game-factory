# Build Log: Tithe

**Status:** deployed

## Idea Source
`IDEAS.md`, the board-game initiative queue — the first entry remaining after skyhold consumed the
Santorini-shaped one earlier the same day (removed on take):

> **Sensei's Gambit** (Onitama-shaped) — a 5×5 duel where the only moves available are the four
> animal cards in play; use one to move a stone, then that card slides to your opponent and theirs
> slides to you, so the toolkit constantly rotates; capture the master or reach the far throne.
> vs 2, lockstep + bot; different fixed card-sets = modes.

The mechanic is reimplemented; the identity is original throughout. The source game is never
referenced in anything shipped.

## The rename, which happened before any code
The working title was **Veer**, with a wind / Hearth / Drift theme. The prior-art agent found the
collision: **frostward** — shipped, *same genre* — already ships a card literally named `Veer`, a
mode id `drift`, and *hearths* as its core defended objects. `veer` was free as a repo name; that
was never the problem. Renamed to **Tithe** (a payment you are compelled to give away, which is
exactly the mechanic), with Keeper / Bearer / Coffer / toll and the seats Ochre and Indigo. The
scaffold directory was renamed and every slug-bearing file rewritten before the rules existed.

## Distinctness from skyhold, which shipped the same day
Both are 5×5 two-player perfect-information abstracts with a minimax bot and lockstep netcode, so
this had to be earned rather than assumed. Skyhold's verb is **build the terrain**: movement rules
are fixed, the board only ever rises, and termination falls out of that. Tithe's verb is **hand your
tool over**: the movement rules are *objects that change hands*, and the board is subtractive. No
other game in the fleet gives the player a hand of *moves*, and none makes spending a resource hand
that resource to the opponent.

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Tithe / ben-gy/tithe
- **Genre:** board
- **Core loop:** spend one of your two tolls to move a piece; the toll you spend goes to the table
  and your opponent picks it up. Win by taking their Keeper or walking yours into their coffer.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, **deterministic lockstep**, exactly 2 seated players (+ spectators)
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM board + toll cards, canvas particle overlay
- **Engine modules used:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+ qr), rng, sound, storage, mobile, identity

## Input question (principle #23)
Asked and answered on purpose: **touch**. A deliberation game on twenty-five squares needs an input
that is precise and re-considerable; a gyroscope cannot pick one square out of twenty-five, and
shouting at a board where precision *is* the product is a novelty tax. The fleet's sensor quota is
well served — warble (pitch), brim (accelerometer), waft (camera), teeter (gyro) and uproar
(loudness) are all recent, roughly one build in three.

## Juice
Pieces hop; captures shake the board and burst (alarm-red for a Bearer, gold and heavier for a
Keeper); paying a toll pings a rising blip; the levy thuds and puffs grey where it collected; a win
flashes the board. Legal destinations lift and ring — a plain ring for a quiet move, a red X-ring for
a capture, so the difference is a *shape* first. Procedural audio, haptics on captures and wins,
`prefers-reduced-motion` honoured, particle pool hard-capped.

## What the simulation caught that playing it would not have
The balance sim was built **first** and refereed every constant. Three findings, all of which
overruled a confident prior:

1. **A wrong-signed evaluation term.** The first eval scored *holding* a strong toll as a good thing
   ("a toll you hold is a toll you're about to hand over"). A control blind to that term then won
   **96% as seat 0 and 96% as seat 1** — the signature of a sign error, not a seat effect. Both
   handover eval weights are now **measured at zero and switched off**: the search already knows what
   the exchange does, because it plays through it at every node. An eval term for it was a second,
   worse copy of information the engine already had.
2. **Paying the cutter twice.** The plan gave the first move to the player who cuts the tolls. That
   handed seat 0 **71%** of Ledger. `FIRST_MOVER` is now the *chooser*, pinned by a test.
3. **No monotone quantity.** Ledger averaged 65–83 turns and hit the old 200-turn backstop in **13%**
   of games. The **levy** replaced it: every `mode.levy` turns without a capture, the rearmost Bearer
   is taken from whoever has *more* pieces standing. Mean length roughly halved (83 → 48), the cap
   became a derivation rather than a guess, and stalling on a lead is now the one thing punished.
   The threefold-repetition "impasse" rule the plan called for was **removed** — it fired 0 times in
   300 games, so it was decoration.

A fourth, about the sim itself: the negative-GAP control was **silently a no-op** at first. `copyInto`
propagates flags from the parent, so searching directly off the real game state reset `frozenTolls`
on the very first copy — and the control arm produced *byte-identical* numbers to the sighted one.
A control that agrees exactly is not strict, it is broken. Fixed by searching from a detached root.

### Numbers
Deep sweep, depth 4, n=50, **two independent seed families**:

| mode | cutter win rate (famA / famB) | mean moves | endings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ledger | 52% / 44% | 48 | collect 57, seize 30, drained 13 |
| Spree | 56% / 46% | 27 | seize 51, collect 49 |
| Arrears | 48% / 54% | 27 | collect 58, seize 42 |

- **Negative GAP:** a bot blind to the *mechanic* (same search, same depth, same eval — it just
  plans as though the toll it pays never reaches the opponent) loses **95–100%**, from either seat.
- **Skill ladder:** search ≫ greedy ≫ random, in every mode.
- **Search speed:** depth 4 ≈ 10–30ms, depth 6 ≈ 130–380ms, depth 7 ≈ 0.7–1.5s. Reckoner is d7 with
  a 900ms cap, so the cap is what binds.

## Three real bugs, each found by a different gate
1. **Contrast (#22):** the chrome gold Keeper ring measured **2.51:1** against the Ochre piece — a
   ring you cannot quite see on the one piece whose capture ends the game. Split into a separate,
   paler `ring` colour, ≥3:1 on both sides. **Mutation-verified** (revert → 2 tests red).
2. **Layout (#20), desktop:** a flat `gap: 24px` on the desktop grid also spaced the seven stacked
   *rows* — 144px of it — pushing the action bar **132px below the fold** at 1280×800 while every
   phone check stayed perfect. Exactly the outsize lesson. Fixed to `gap: 10px 24px` and the board
   chrome budget re-derived. **Mutation-verified** (revert → red).
3. **Found only by driving the UI:** in Arrears a move can ask two questions in a row (which toll do
   you pay, then which do you take off the table) and the second reused the first overlay — so the
   prompt read "take from the table" while the player was looking at *their own hand*. The overlay is
   now stamped with which question it is asking and re-renders when that changes.

## The mechanism audit (principle #21)
Re-derives the deck offsets **by hand**, plus the split enumeration, the ply format, the exchange,
the levy and the win conditions. `genPlies`, `step` and `TOLLS` are deliberately **not** imported —
only the seeded deal is taken from the game, because re-deriving that would mean re-implementing the
PRNG and proves nothing about the rules. Run over the sim's ply stream at zero tolerance across
modes × policies: `illegalOffset`, `friendlyFire`, `ghostMover`, `brokenHandoff`, `tollsLost`,
`reclaimed`, `freePass`, `winNotEnded`, `falseWin`, `overran` — **all 0**, with positive controls
proving each counter can fire (a shifted destination → `illegalOffset`; a move relabelled as a slack
ply → `freePass`; a doctored winner or reason → `falseWin`; random play → `missedWin > 0`).

`missedWin` is split from `winNotEnded` on purpose. "A player did not spot a win" is bot quality —
random play does it constantly, which is exactly what makes it the positive control. "A win was
*played* and the round carried on" is the rules failing, and that is the zero-tolerance one.

## Errors & Resolutions
- **`mulberry32` is not an engine export** — it is `makeRng`. Caught by the first typecheck.
- **`frozenTolls` wiped by `copyInto`** — see above; the control arm was a silent no-op.
- **Two test-side bugs, not code bugs:** piece *indices* are not mirrored between the sides (the
  occupied *sets* are), and `-dx` yields `-0`, which fails strict deep equality. `orient()` now
  normalises negative zero away, which is free and prevents the same surprise downstream.
- **A rules path mistaken for a bug:** a helper failed to submit a move because seat 0, holding a
  backward-only hand on its home row, genuinely had no legal move — the becalmed rule firing
  correctly. The helper now plays the slack ply.
- The final synthesis agent proposed a wholesale redesign (new name, 24-card deck, different modes).
  It was not taken: the design in hand had measured evidence behind it and the rebuild had none. The
  one finding it raised that my own sim independently confirmed — no monotone potential — *was*
  adopted, as the levy.

## Honest caveats
- Ledger still ends ~13% of games `drained` (the levy having stripped the board). That is now a
  designed, legible ending with a stated tie-break rather than an arbitrary turn cap, but it is the
  dullest of the three outcomes and is called out as such rather than smoothed over.
- The shipped `balance.test.ts` runs at a deliberately shallow depth so it fits in `npm test` (~48s).
  Its bounds are wide on purpose; the deep sweep that actually chose the constants is the table above.
- The pie-cut counter-control asserts the cut pulls the result *toward even*, not that it favours a
  particular seat — which side an arbitrary deal flatters depends on engine strength, so a
  directional test would pass or fail on the search budget rather than on the rule.
- Both smoke-test tabs showed the same handle because two tabs of one browser share localStorage;
  real separate devices get distinct random handles.
