# Build Log: Clatter
**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Status:** deployed

## Idea Source
IDEAS.md, first entry in the board-game initiative queue (now removed):

> **Dice Genius** (Ganz schön clever / "That's Pretty Clever"-shaped) — on your turn roll six dice,
> pick one (everything lower goes to the opponents to use), and slot it into one of five interlocking
> colour tracks that chain-trigger each other into satisfying combo cascades. parallel same-seed race
> 2-6; host rolls; solo daily score-attack.

Mechanic taken, identity not: original name, original tracks, original scoring, original art, and the
source game is referenced nowhere in the shipped product.

**The input question (principle #23) was asked and answered "touch, on purpose."** The last three
builds (skyhold, tacit, tithe) are all touch board/card games, which is the signal to reach for a
sensor — but the fleet already ships mic (warble, uproar), accelerometer (brim), gyro (teeter) and
camera (waft), and a roll-and-write is a *reading* game whose decision is arithmetic over a sheet.
Shaking a phone to roll would have been a costume on top of it rather than an input.

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Clatter / ben-gy/clatter
- **Genre:** card (the fleet's "cards & dice" bucket). The fleet's **first dice game and first
  roll-and-write** in 50 shipped games.
- **Core loop:** take one die from the pool; every die strictly smaller than it spills to everybody
  else; write yours into one of four interlocking tracks whose marked cells cascade free marks into
  the others.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, 2–4. **Lockstep over a replicated action log with no host.**
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM
- **Engine modules:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+ qr), rng, sound, storage, mobile, identity

## The design panel
Ran a 10-agent workflow: three independent rulesets from three different angles, each judged by two
adversarial lenses (fun/depth and implementability/mobile), then a synthesis. **The judges scored the
three designs 3, 5 and 5** and were worth every token — the flaws they found shaped the shipped game:

- Every candidate resolved the passive picks as a **blocking N−1 prompt chain around the table**,
  which is phone-hostile and P2P-hostile. Clatter's passive picks touch only their own sheet, so they
  commute and happen **simultaneously**.
- Every candidate had a **cascade with player-facing choices**, which is an unlogged control-flow
  branch and a desync surface. Clatter's free marks resolve to a **fixed deterministic target**.
- Two candidates' phone budgets were **arithmetically wrong** for a real iOS viewport.
- One candidate's bot was measured (by the judge, in node) to blow the sim budget by 2–3 orders.

Two ideas were grafted from the synthesis: **pairs-to-seven** self-documenting geometry (a pair
opened with a 2 prints the 5 it wants), and paying the active player for the dice that did *not*
spill — which the balance sim then killed outright (below).

## Juice
Procedural clatter on the roll, a rising pitch per free mark so a cascade sounds like a combo, a
gold flash + shake + haptic on completing a track, pop animation on every mark, and the tray marking
the dice that are **about to spill while the player is still holding the die** — the teaching moment
and the best bit of feedback in the game. All degrade under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## What the simulation killed (principle #18, and the reason to build the sim first)
1. **A shim worth 2 points.** Declining the spill paid better than most placements, so the bots
   declined all game: at four players they finished with **7 of 32 cells filled**. Fixed by making a
   shim worth 1 AND making declining illegal when anything is legal. Mutation-verified: the original
   build fails the fill-rate assertion.
2. **Paying for the dice that did NOT spill** (the synthesis's idea). It paid you to switch the
   signature mechanic off — the spill was empty on **63–91% of turns**. Replaced by Tribute mode,
   which pays for **uptake** instead, and the mechanic now fires on 57–93% of turns.
3. **An "Updraft" mode** (high dice spill) was cut: taking the top die was simultaneously best for
   you *and* starved the table, so the mode had no tension at all (spill empty 71–91%).
4. **Scramble's "a finished track scores double"** made the leader after 4 of 12 turns win 70%.
   Replaced by "only your best two tracks count" — same question, none of the swing. Blowouts went
   from 47% to 0%.
5. **The last seat won 39% of four-player games.** Being active late means taking from a full pool
   while your own sheet is most constrained — worth ~1 point per seat step, invisible until you
   count it. **Two structural fixes were tried and BOTH OVERSHOT**: reversing the order at half-time
   took seat 0 from 17% to 35%, and rotating the starting seat was worse still at 2P (64/36). The
   shipped fix pays the bias off in points, swept 0..4 per mode **and per player count** — a single
   number per mode could not fit (Scramble at 2P wants zero compensation, the same mode at 3–4P
   wants two). Mean score per seat is now within **0.08–1.60 points** across all nine combinations.

**A broken control arm nearly hid #2.** The first attempt to disable the keep changed `step()` but
not the bot's evaluator, and the two arms produced **byte-identical** numbers — which is the tell
that a control is broken, not strict.

### Bugs the gates caught that a screenshot could not
- **A filled cell painted itself sheet-on-sheet at 1.0:1** — `background: currentColor` next to
  `color: var(--sheet)` resolves the background against the *new* colour. The written number was in
  the DOM, in the right cell, at the right size, and invisible; the screenshot looked fine. Found by
  measuring the computed styles, fixed with a per-track `--tc`, pinned by a source guard,
  mutation-verified red.
- **A false desync on a rival claim.** When two peers both filled in for one silent seat, the peer
  holding the *winning* action compared digests against a log it had just discarded and faulted
  `'desync'` — killing the round at the exact moment the protocol was working. Found by the takeover
  test, fixed, mutation-verified red.
- **A `<span class="die">` is inline**, so width/height did nothing and every die in prose rendered
  as a 2px sliver.
- **Duplicate faces rendered as dead dice** — `legalActions` offers one action per distinct face, so
  the second 4 in a pool looked untouchable.

## Errors & Resolutions
- `createSfx(muted, opts)` is a one-argument API — passed a config object instead.
- The manifest test inherited a sibling's convention (`background_color === theme_color`). Clatter's
  theme colour is deliberately the gold accent while the page is near-black, so the assertion was
  rewritten to the thing that actually matters: the splash background must equal the CSS `--ground`.
- The seat-fairness assertion was rewritten around **mean score per seat** rather than win rate. The
  same shipped build measured 11.6pp, 4.1pp and 2.7pp of seat deviation under three seed schemes at
  300 games — the win rate is simply too coarse a statistic at that sample, while mean score sees the
  identical effect with a standard error under a point.

## Not verified
- The two-peer P2P smoke test ran against the **local preview**, not production. Production was
  loaded, played and screenshotted in-browser at both widths, but the multi-tab run was local.
- No real-device check (a phone in a hand) is possible in this pipeline.
