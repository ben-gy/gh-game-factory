**The idea's central technical claim is false, and that was the run's main finding** — see below.

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Shunt / ben-gy/shunt
- **Genre:** puzzle
- **Core loop:** tap a road to propel the rake in, tap a wagon to draw it out; every trip is one
  move because every trip is a change of direction. The loco only works the near end, so the order
  of a rake never reverses — that single constraint is the whole puzzle.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, 2–6, **parallel same-seed race with no authoritative host**. Each peer
  generates the identical yard from the frozen round seed; only `{moves, seated, undos}` and a final
  score cross the wire.
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM + CSS
- **Engine modules:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+ QR), rng, storage, sound, mobile, identity.
  Deliberately not used: loop (turn-based), input/joystick/drag (every move has one parameter).

## The finding: par had to be computed, not walked
The queued idea proposes generating by walking a finished yard backwards and calling the walk length
par. **A backwards walk yields *a* solution, never the shortest** — it doubles back on itself
constantly. Shipping it would have made every results screen a lie, telling players they were "+3
over par" on yards they had in fact beaten, which is precisely the promise the idea wanted to make.

Replaced with: generation and par are **one Dijkstra outward from every finished position**, valid
because push and pull are exact inverses (so the move graph is undirected with symmetric costs).
One pass yields the exact optimum for every reachable position, a supply of openings at any
difficulty, and the optimal line for the replay.

**Measured rather than assumed** (this decided the mode sizes):

| Mode | reachable states | whole-space search | worst generation | par band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sidings (6 wagons, train 4) | 13,286 | 20ms | 13ms | 7–11 (mean 8.87) |
| Run-round (7 wagons, train 5) | 85,780 | 100ms | 50ms | 11–15 (mean 12.73) |
| Kickback (7 wagons, train 4) | 8,592 | 8ms | 7ms | 13–17 (mean 14.73) |

A real parity property fell out: **openings exist only at odd distances** (the loco starts light and
must finish having propelled a train in), which is why the bands step by two.

The claim is audited, not trusted: an independent forward search (`optimalParDirect`, sharing only
`rules.ts`) agrees with par on every seed of every mode, and a negative control proves no solution
exists at `par − 1`.

## Errors & Resolutions
1. **Wagons overflowed their own road** once the loco arrived — the road was drawn to its wagon
   capacity, but the loco stands at the mouth while it works. Fixed to `(cap + 1) * slot`, pinned by
   a layout test.
2. **Board marooned in a tall phone / dead half on desktop** — rows now grow to the height offered
   (wagons keep their proportions and centre), and the board is sized to its content and centred.
   Measuring the parent rather than the host avoids a shrink-on-every-resize feedback loop.
3. **The live rival counter never updated** (found by the two-peer smoke test). Rival status arrived
   and updated state but repainted only the *results* screen, so in a race you watched a frozen
   counter until you moved yourself. The wire was fine; the repaint was missing. Fixed by driving
   the game screen from `onChange` too; regression test added.
4. **A stale `?room=` teleported you back into a room you had left** (principle #11). `enterRoom`
   puts the code in the URL so the tab can be reloaded in-room, but nothing cleared it on the way
   out. Fixed with a `clearRoomInUrl()` on leave plus a once-per-page-load latch on the invite link;
   both pinned by tests.

## Verification notes (honest)
- **Layout was verified by screenshot plus an arithmetic geometry test, not by scripted
  `getBoundingClientRect`.** The automation pane reports `document.visibilityState === 'hidden'`
  even when fronted, and Chrome then returns stale rects for transform-positioned elements — the
  rects claimed every wagon sat at the origin while the screenshot showed them correctly placed, and
  a manually applied transform also failed to register. Real taps and real paints are correct
  (verified by driving actual clicks through the shipped path and by looking at every mode at both
  widths). Overflow was checked via `documentElement.scrollWidth`, which is unaffected.
- **Move counts and times are self-reported.** A friendly P2P game has no referee; no serverless
  design can change that.
