## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** bracken / ben-gy/bracken
- **Genre:** board
- **Core loop:** on your turn, either walk one square towards the far edge or plant a hedge to
  lengthen a rival's route — never one that leaves anybody with no route at all.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, 2–4, **lockstep with no authority at all**
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM
- **Engine modules used:** net, rematch, turn, lobby, identity, sound, storage, mobile (+ mobile.css)

**Name.** Checked three ways: free on GitHub, absent from registry/index, and absent from every

**Balance (principle #18).** Bot beats the shortest-route control **12/12 on every mode**. Ladder is
real: keeper/rambler 12/12, poacher/rambler 11/12, keeper/poacher 10/12. **P(leader at ply N wins):
fold 50% @6, 54% @12, 77% @20, 92% @30; heath 50% @8, 51% @20, 75% @34, 92% @50** — flat and near
chance through the opening, spiking only at the end, which is the curve the principle asks for. Every
mode × 2/3/4 runners terminates well inside its ply cap (worst: heath/4p mean 109, max 162 of 400).

**Mechanism audit (principle #21).** 270 games / **17,735 plies**: `sealedRunners=0
illegalHedges=0 phantomSteps=0 supplyLeaks=0`. The auditor calls **none** of the game's legality
functions and never reads `state.walls` — it rebuilds the board from `state.placed` as a set of
unordered cell-pair keys, a different representation from the shipped bitmask, so a wrong direction
bit in `sever` would surface as a disagreement rather than be rubber-stamped. **Every detector is
mutation-verified and those controls are kept as real tests**; the strongest plays a real 8-hedge
position, asserts it audits clean, then walls it off by hand and asserts the *same* auditor reports
`sealedRunners=2, illegalHedges=10` on a board it had just certified.

**Contrast (principle #22).** Every meaningful colour ≥3:1 against board, turf *and* lattice — worst
measured **4.18:1** (runnerC on lattice); ink on every runner ≥7.9:1; text ≥8:1 on every panel. The
test also parses `:root` in the stylesheet and asserts it matches `palette.ts`, so a colour cannot
exist only in the CSS where nothing can pin it.

## Errors & Resolutions

**1. The `log` channel was torn down at round start — every multiplayer move went into a void.**
Its `off()` was registered via `onCleanup`, but that list is **per screen** and `startNetRound` calls
`teardown()` — so the receiver detached at exactly the moment the round began. The host's move
applied locally and the guest's log stayed empty. Found by the two-peer smoke test; **no unit test
would have caught it**, because the fake bus sits above the real channel wiring. Fixed by making the
channel room-scoped (`logOff`, called only in `leaveRoom`). This is the documented
"room objects aren't per-screen" trap, hit exactly as described. After the fix both peers converged
on identical ply logs, board and shared pool, in both directions.

**2. A 38px tap target at 375px.** `layout.test.ts` failed on its own headline invariant: the worst
legal-step pair on a 9×9 at 375px measures **38px**, under the 44px floor. My assumption that legal
steps are never adjacent was wrong — the jump-and-bend rule around a rival puts a bend target
diagonally beside an ordinary side-step, one cell apart. A 9×9 board **cannot** give 44px cells in
375px (it would need 436px), so the fix had to be in the interaction, not the geometry:
`needsConfirm` detects the crowded case and aims-then-confirms, leaving the ordinary case a single
tap. The test now asserts the pairing directly — every step set that breaches the floor must be one
`needsConfirm` flags.

**3. Coppice showed "12 hedges" against *each* runner**, which reads as twelve each and hides the
entire point of the shared stack. The HUD now prints the stack once as its own row.

**4. The engine's room-entry and lobby markup was completely unstyled** — flush to the viewport edge
in default UA grey. The engine ships the markup and each game supplies the skin; that block was
missing. Also fixed a cascade in my first attempt at it: `.re-head` is a *container*, and styling it
as a heading rendered the subtitle at 26px bold.

## What the measurements changed
First-move advantage, measured before anything was designed around it:

| board | n=60 (first pass) | n=300–600, two independent seed families |
|---|---|---|
| 7×7, 6 each | 65.0% | **51.2% ±4.0** (n=600) |
| 7×7, second player +1 hedge | 46.7% | **41.7% ±2.8** (n=300) |
| 9×9, 10 each | 45.0% | **47.5% ±4.0** (n=600) |
| 9×9 shared stack | — | **47.7% ±5.7** (n=300) |

The n=60 pass said the small board was badly unfair and that an extra hedge for the second player
fixed it. **Both halves were wrong.** The even game is already fair, and the "fix" would have made
the *second* player the favourite. Shipping on the first number would have introduced the exact
unfairness it was meant to remove — so **bracken ships with no compensation rule**. During
calibration single n=300 families read 40.0%, 48.0%, 48.7%, 48.7%, 49.3% and 53.7%; the shipped test
therefore uses two families, prints each CI, and asserts on the pooled rate.
