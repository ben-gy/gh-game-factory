## The three modes, and how the third one got fixed before it was built
WHAT / WHERE / WHEN. **Setwork** (printed mosaic — one legal cell per course, so denial is exact),
**Freehand** (blank mosaic; the column is nominated on the load that first fills a course, which keeps
round-end resolution fully automatic), **Quickfire** (courses lay the instant they fill, mid-turn, and
the game ends the moment a row completes — mid-round, with everyone's unlaid courses stranded).

An adversarial pass **killed the first Quickfire**: "lays instantly then locks" was *proven* to be
Setwork plus a 1–3 point ordering wrinkle — the row restriction cannot tighten mid-round because the
course that would tighten it is the one that just locked, and overflow already lands immediately in
every mode. The **instant end trigger** is what makes WHEN a real axis.

## Rules holes closed before a line of renderer
The same pass found a **non-termination path** assembled from two omissions: nothing said the Spoil
was swept at round end (so the ladder would be cumulative for the whole game *and* tiles would leave
circulation monotonically), and nothing defined a **short deal** (bag and discard both exhausted).
Together: a 4P game that eventually deals nothing and loops empty rounds until a backstop fires. Both
are now defined and both are tested directly rather than hoped for. Also closed: Freehand load
legality now requires a legal column to exist (row- and column-legality could otherwise disagree and
open a sheet with five dimmed chips and no way out); the 0 floor is applied once, at round end, in all
modes; the Trowel stays with its holder if nobody takes it; ties break on complete rows, then fewest
tiles spoiled, then seat.

## The sim (built first, and it refereed)
Node-capped at 400 for reproducibility, n=32/18/16 by table size, 3 modes × {2,3,4}P, ~64s.
- Every game ends on a completed row; the 15-round backstop **never fires** under a bot that is trying.
- **Seat fairness** on mean SCORE: max deviation 3.9, inside the second-family trigger and inside SEs
  of 1.1–3.7. No seat bias declared.
- **Drama:** leader after round 1 wins 51.6% in Setwork. Blowout rate 0–3.1%.
- **Mode differentiation is itself a gate** and it passes: mean score 30.2 / 36.6 / 23.5.
- **Negative gap, decisive in all three modes:** the control (identical search, identical node cap,
  identical *transition function*, rival+sill weights zeroed at the eval call site every ply) loses at
  **68.8% / 87.5% / 75.0%**, score gaps **+7.9 / +14.5 / +10.2** (SE 2.4–2.7). Both positive controls
  fire; the arms play different games on 12/12 seeds.

**Honest nulls, reported rather than buried.** The narrow arm (W_SILL alone zeroed) measures 55% at
n=20 — *not resolvable* at any sample this suite can afford; the search already sees the exchange, so
what is load-bearing is caring about rivals at all, not that one weight. At 4P the sighted bot wins
37.5% against a 25% baseline at n=24 (SE ≈9.9pp, ≈1.3σ) — weaker by construction, because what you
leave feeds a pot rather than a chosen rival. Stated as the finding, not tuned until it moved.

**A measured property worth knowing:** every game runs **exactly five rounds**. Five lays in one row
is the minimum for any row and a competent player always takes it, so the end trigger is a floor
rather than a race — which is precisely what makes Quickfire's instant end the interesting axis.

## The bot
Once the racks are dealt a round is **perfect information with zero chance nodes**, so the horizon is
the round boundary and `Table` has **no bag in it at all** — clairvoyance is a type error. Measured at
a full round start: **b=28 (2P) / 39 (4P), depth 5 at 2P and 3–5 at 4P, 141k / 61k nodes/s.** The
candidate budget that took b from 81 to 39 is worth a whole ply. `W_TUMBLE` is an O(5) dot product,
never a nested search over opponent replies.

## Errors & Resolutions
Six real defects, four of them found by looking and playing rather than by a test:
1. **Latent, found by the audit:** `printedKind` and `printedCol` were two independent closed forms
   nothing forced to agree — the renderer drew its hint from one and the rules laid the tile with the
   other, so flipping one changed nothing the rules could see. `printedCol` is now derived from
   `printedKind` and precomputed.
2. **Desktop layout:** the sill was pinned to the *bottom* of a full-height grid row, ~600px below the
   racks it belongs to, with the whole middle of the screen empty. Rows now size to content and the
   draft column is two real rows.
3. **Desktop, caught by the layout test before a browser saw it:** side columns pinned at 400+300
   squeezed the centre to 244px at exactly 1000px wide and the cell fell to 23px.
4. **Copy:** the sheet's tumble line read *"leaves ◆×1 in the sill → Kestrel, Vane, Rook takes it from
   Kestrel, Vane, Rook"* — at round start every rival can legally use every kind, so "could use it"
   was replaced by "has already committed to it", and the two clauses became two sentences.
5. **Results screen:** it handed its whole container to `createLobby`, which owns its container's
   innerHTML and wiped the everyone's-outcome breakdown the screen exists to show. The lobby now has
   its own slot.
6. **Rematch:** the controls were simply absent because `rounds.finish()` was never called, so the
   engine's lobby correctly painted nothing while the round phase was still `playing` and "Play again"
   voted into a void.

Also added during the smoke test: a **digest** `(rules revision, mode, players, seed)` on the wire.
Replaying a ply log is only a *probabilistic* mismatch detector — a short log of moves legal under both
rulebooks replays perfectly, and a 2-player log was happily rebuilt as a 3-player game.
