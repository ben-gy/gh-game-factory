## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Haggle / ben-gy/haggle
- **Genre:** card
- **Core loop:** take a ware, hire the porters, barter, or sell — and a sale pays the top chits off a
  column that only ever falls, so every turn is "bank a small sale now, or hold while my rival drains
  the column I am waiting on".
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, 2 players, host-authoritative (both satchels and the crate order are
  hidden). Channels `hag` / `hageye`, both ≤12 bytes. A full redacted snapshot per recipient every
  time — never a delta.
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM
- **Engine modules:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+ qr), rng, sound, storage, mobile + mobile.css

## Juice
Chits arc from the column to the score with a 40ms stagger and a `setTimeout` backstop alongside the
transition (a backgrounded tab must never strand one). Procedural SFX: a soft click on select, a
rising arpeggio on a sale, a bright flourish on a bounty, a dull struck note for Brisk's bell, a flat
dead note when a column runs dry. `LAST` columns take a dashed amber ring and the literal word; `DRY`
takes a 45° hatch and the word. `prefers-reduced-motion` collapses every duration to 1ms.

## What the sim found (it was built and run BEFORE the interface)

**1. The bell's cadence was unfair, and the obvious cadence was the unfair one.** Ringing once per
turn-pair puts the *same* seat immediately before every bell. Measured across three independent seed
families at n=50: mover win rate **44% / 34% / 44%**, ~2 chits of deficit, the same direction every
time. A period **coprime to the seat count** rotates who stands beside it; three is the smallest.
Pooled mover Δ went to **+0.37** and the sign now flips between families. Pinned by a test.

**2. The evaluation was mis-tuned, and the control arm is what said so.** The design discounted a
holding as its column drained, plus a supply-conditioned term to rescue that. With both live the bot
**lost to its own control arms in Dearth — 22% and 46%.** A bot that loses to a deliberately
misinformed copy of itself is not measuring a mode; it is wrong. Removing both improved the price gap
in **every** mode (70→77, 92→96, 46→58). Re-measured against the shipped bot, each restored weight
now lands inside noise, so the shipped statement is the honest one — **not resolvable, therefore ship
the simpler evaluation** — and both arms stay in the suite so re-deriving the same plausible idea
turns something red.

**3. Dearth's price gap is an honest null and is reported as one.** TARIFFBLIND (byte-identical
search, depth, beam, belief, tuning and transition; one seam changed) loses **77%** in Long Haul
(margin +10.3) and **96%** in Brisk (+15.2) — and **58% ±7.0pp** in Dearth. Dearth ships **without
the depleting-price claim**: its axis was always *when supply arrives*, and skill there is proven by
the tier ladder instead (Chandler beats Hawker **90%**).

Other measured numbers, all with standard errors, from n=44 per mode plus three seed families for
fairness: plies 30.4 / 24.4 / 32.7 · mean score 46.0 / 33.5 / 45.3 · blowouts (≥25) 6.8 / 0 / 2.3% ·
within 4 chits 22.7 / 54.5 / 15.9% · leader at 20% of the deal wins 52 / 59 / 46%, at 85% wins 84 /
85 / 74% · verbs take 35–39%, sell 35–37%, hire 14–17%, barter 9–13%, **dump 0.0%** · legal moves a
turn 8.0 / 8.6 / 4.5 with the best within 1.5 of the second-best on 60–72% of turns · the two
scripted lines a real player reaches for first (hoard the cheap ware; always cut the dearest column)
lose 93–100% in every mode.

## Bugs the gates found
1. **The two seat colours measured 1.11:1 against each other** — fine on a colour screen, identical
   in greyscale, on the one element that says who is winning. Now 2.23:1 apart in luminance, and the
   HUD labels them in words as well.
2. **The compact row set (522px) cannot fit an 812×375 landscape phone**, however hard it is
   squeezed. Landscape is now a genuine two-column grid, in a query that ships after every width
   query — an 812px-wide phone matches the tablet rule, and whichever is written later wins.
3. **The barter event named the wares it took but not the berths**, so a replay could not be exact
   when two berths held the same ware. Found by the audit disagreeing with the game; the berths are
   public and are now on the wire.
4. The audit could not reconstruct the opening table at all until a `deal` event was added — the
   trestle is face-up, so it is public by rule and leaks nothing.

   quiet-zoned, measured inside the lobby card with zero document overflow, captioned "Scan to join

2. **Host stickiness:** the creator kept host; the guest adopted it, saw the host's gossiped **Dearth**
   on a disabled picker reading "The host picks the market", and did not steal it.
3. **In sync:** identical public tables on both peers, each holding its own **different** satchel;
   play in both directions to an identical final state (ply 35, chits 42–30, ended `dry`), with the
   results screen showing **both** players' per-ware breakdowns and 57 of 129 chits recorded unclaimed.
4. **Rematch:** both tapped Play again → a fresh **deal 2** in the same room, new trestle, scores
   reset, both present, the pennant tally persisted, and the **loser of deal 1 starting deal 2**
   exactly as the fairness rule says.
5. **Zoom:** `touch-action: manipulation` on body and stage, `gesturestart` prevented, scale 1.
   **Stale `?room=`:** leaving cleared the URL and the next "play with a friend" offered create/join.
6. **Host leave mid-deal:** the survivor did **not** freeze — it closed the deal as a **walkover**
   with "Your rival left the room", reached a real results screen with both breakdowns, the match
   tally intact at 2–0, and both "New match" and "Back to the lobby" live.

**A host takeover is deliberately not implemented, and the reason is stated rather than hidden.** The
crate is hidden from *everybody*, so unlike a game with no draw pile a promoted peer has nothing to
re-attest against, and a fresh shuffle would silently change which wares are still coming — the single
most important fact in the game. A room collapsing to one player is therefore a walkover, armed from
**both** `onPeerLeave` and 20s of heartbeat silence.
