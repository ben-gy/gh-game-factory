# Build Log: Weald

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Status:** deployed
**URL:** https://weald.benrichardson.dev · **Repo:** https://github.com/ben-gy/weald

## Idea Source

First line of IDEAS.md's board-game initiative queue, taken verbatim and removed from the queue:

> **Mapwright** (Cartographers-shaped) — each season, draw the revealed terrain shape anywhere on your
> map to satisfy that season's two scoring edicts; ambush cards force everyone to carve a monster in.
> parallel same-seed 2-6; edict decks = modes.

**Renamed to Weald.** "Mapwright" is a direct English synonym for the source game's title, so it
fails the identity rule even though it collides with nothing. Three of the four design agents
independently proposed "Vellum"; I chose Weald instead because Vellum is also a commercial
book-formatting app, "weald" has zero hits across all 56 shipped games' `src/` (including the
bundled word lists), and it was free on GitHub. Renaming later would also have forced a second Pages
CNAME cycle, which the fleet's own notes record as wedging TLS issuance for over an hour.

## Prior art

A dedicated agent read all 56 registry entries and grepped the fleet. Not a duplicate. Nearest
neighbours and what Weald has that they do not:

- **kiln** — per-player grid scored by adjacency, but its supply is contested and it lays exactly
  one tile per round; nothing is ever drawn as a multi-cell shape.
- **clatter** — the fleet's only roll-and-write, but four linear tracks with no 2D geometry and
  fixed printed scoring.
- **wordloom** — the same parallel same-seed netcode into a private grid, but the atom is one cell,
  the scoring rule is fixed, and there is no shared adverse event.
- **ballast / kilter** — the only shipped polyomino games, both real-time falling-block stackers.

Three organs the fleet did not have: **free polyomino placement as the committed action**;
**scoring rules revealed as cards and varying per run**; and **a shared adverse event every seat
absorbs simultaneously**, resolved privately, where the skill is *where* you take identical damage.

## Game Details

- **Genre / stack / render:** board · vanilla-ts · DOM
- **Core loop:** everyone is dealt the same shape; draw it anywhere on your own map; the edicts
  decide what it was worth.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, 1–6, **parallel same-seed**. No game state on the wire at all — every
  peer derives crags, edict roles, cards and carves from the frozen seed. Only the finished grid
  crosses, once per watch, so every summary can show every player's map.
- **Engine modules:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+ QR), rng, sound, storage, mobile.

## How the design was produced (ultracode)

A 17-agent workflow, all pinned to Opus 5: prior-art check → four independent designs (purist /
tension / mobile / solo) → five judge panels → synthesis → five adversarial refuters → final spec.
1.58M subagent tokens, 49 minutes. The mobile-angle design won on phone-playability (9/10) and
buildability (8/10) and became the spine.

**The refuters earned their keep.** All five returned DO_NOT_SHIP, and one finding was decisive and
correct: the vertical budget was computed against a height the phone never gives you. My chrome is
320px and a 375px-wide board is 359px — 679px total, against roughly 553px of visible viewport on an
iPhone SE. The board had to be sized from available **height**, not width. Fixed and verified: at
360×553 the map shrinks to 255px and the Place button sits at y=550. Without it the commit control
was ~90px below the fold and the game was unfinishable on the most common small phone. A second
refuter finding — no landscape layout at all — was also real and is fixed with a two-column grid.

I **rejected** the "endgame is arithmetically bookkeeping" critique: it reasoned about the
synthesised spec's schedule, and measurement contradicts it (watch totals rise 11.8 → 15.3 → 15.5 →
21.4, and the median legal-option count never drops below 20).

## Balance: measured, not argued

Built the sim first and let it referee. The **first** run of it found things no amount of reasoning
would have:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| March mean score | 44.0, **p5 = −4** | 56.4, p5 = 32 |
| March canker bill | −49.5/run | −22.9/run |
| Canopy edict spread | 27× (Spinney 2.1 vs Cloister 57) | 1.49× |
| March edict spread | 19× | 1.58× |
| Survey edict spread | 10× | 1.74× |

March's fifth-percentile score was **negative** — its doubled canker rate swamped the mode and its
own counterplay (Quarantine) paid 5.4. Three rule thresholds and eight multipliers moved because of
this, and the mode blurb still claiming "the canker bleeds double" was caught by an assertion.

Controls, both green: a bot optimising the **wrong mode's** pool loses **48–79%** (modes are
different games, not a re-skin); locking the bot to one orientation costs **7.8 / 11.8 / 17.4%**
(free rotation is load-bearing), and the arms are asserted non-identical so a broken control flag
fails rather than reads as an inert mechanic.

## Three bugs the gates caught that a screenshot would not

1. **Eleven of fifteen cards printed a number they did not pay.** After the sim retuned the
   multipliers the card texts did not follow — Quarters said "Score 3" and paid 8. In a game that is
   entirely "read the rule, plan toward it", the rules were lying. Found by the mechanism audit,
   which re-derives scoring from the printed English; now pinned by `rules.test.ts`, which parses
   the number back out of the sentence.
2. **A CSS class collision made a button unreachable.** The placement preview was `.ghost`, which is
   also the fleet's button modifier (`.btn.ghost`), so `position:absolute` took the results screen's
   Menu button out of the flex column and stacked it underneath "Play again" at identical
   coordinates. Renamed `.pghost`; guarded at source level.
3. **The solo summary was empty.** With no roster the local player had no seat, so the first watch
   sheet read "0 maps". Bots also only posted a final grid, so their rows had no map.

Each fix was **mutation-verified**: reverted, confirmed the test goes red, restored.

## Test Results

**459 tests across 17 files, all passing.** `npm run build` clean, `tsc --noEmit` clean.

- geometry (33), grid primitives (26), rules + card-text drift (36), determinism (10)
- **balance.test.ts (42)** — seed comparability across two independent seed families, cells
  invariant, difficulty curve, boredom gate, edict spread, cross-play matrix, two negative-gap
  controls, playability
- **mechanism.test.ts (11)** — an independent audit that imports none of the game's maths and
  re-derives every run from the event log: bounds, disjointness, shape congruence under its own
  dihedral group, connectivity, forced-scrawl legitimacy, anchor compliance and exact score. Plus
  **eight mutation checks** proving it is not a rubber stamp.
- contrast (63), layout invariants (23), manifest, and the five P2P contract gates (room code,
  one-join, host election, no-deadlock, rematch, takeover).

Palette respaced after the contrast gate found two terrains **0.0008** apart in luminance (invisible
in greyscale, on the two squares you most need to tell apart). Smallest gap is now 0.080.

## Verification

**Local**, every mode at 375×812, 1280×800, 812×375 landscape and 360×553:
no horizontal overflow anywhere, every run playable to completion, zero sub-44px targets.

**Production** (https://weald.benrichardson.dev), in-browser:

- All three modes played to a finished summary at 375px. Painted-pixel contrast of every terrain
  against an empty square: **4.85–6.3:1** (floor is 3:1).
- Desktop 1280×800: board centred at 520px, 52px cells, no sprawl.
- Overlay gate: `elementFromPoint` at the board centre returns a cell, no stray backdrops, no filter
  on the stage.
- Dismissal contract: ✕, Escape, and an outside tap delivered as real pointer events all close by
  computed state.

**Not verified, stated plainly:** the two-tab P2P smoke test was not run. The netcode is parallel
same-seed, so there is no shared simulation to desync and the automated gates cover the protocol
(one-join invariant, host election, rematch, no-deadlock, round-id rejection, malformed-grid
rejection) — but a live two-browser join, QR scan and rematch was **not** performed this run and
should not be read as having passed.

## Errors & Resolutions

- **Board sized from width alone** → commit button below the fold on a 360×553 phone. Fixed with a
  `min()` of the width and height budgets; mutation-verified.
- **No landscape layout** → at 812×375 the stack needed 680px of height. Fixed with a two-column
  grid, ordered last in the cascade.
- **Card text / weight drift** (11 cards), **`.ghost` collision**, **empty solo summary** — see above.
- Two balance assertions I wrote were themselves wrong and the data corrected them: the cells bound
  omitted carves, and "March pays a bigger canker bill" is false *because the mode works* — its pool
  pays for sealing the rot, so its bot walls it in. The assertion now measures the counterplay.
