## The duplication ruling (this run's first real question)

`weald` shipped three days earlier with the **same netcode shape** (parallel same-seed) and the
**same surface** ("everyone draws the identical thing on their own grid"), so this went to a
three-lens prior-art panel plus a deciding judge *before any code was written*, with EXPANSION as an
acceptable outcome.

Verdict **BUILD**, on evidence rather than taste: weald's `board.ts:57` makes `canPlace` nothing but
an occupancy predicate over a list of cell offsets. Conduit's piece is a **typed edge signature** and
its legality is a constraint over four neighbouring half-edges — not expressible in weald's data
model, renderer or scorer, so logging it as a weald mode would have been logging a lie. Nothing in 58
registry entries scores a per-player grid by connectivity.

The ruling was conditional and the conditions were binding: typed half-edge legality as the headline
organ, connectivity scoring, an independent mechanism audit, three modes on real rule axes, a
negative-gap proof, and zero railway vocabulary. All were built.

**Two things the panel rejected were cut before the scaffold existed:** "special-die expansions =
modes" (a level pack by the factory's own definition) and the pitch's total absence of player
interaction.

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Conduit / ben-gy/conduit
- **Genre:** puzzle
- **Core loop:** four identical fittings a round; choose which to spend and where; sides must match
  exactly, nothing may leave the plate except at a vent of its own gauge, everything must splice.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, parallel same-seed, 2–6
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM + inline SVG
- **Engine modules:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+qr), rng, sound, storage, mobile

## THE SIM OVERRULED THE DESIGN FOUR TIMES

Every constant in `src/modes.ts` is one a sweep landed on. Four confident design ideas died:

1. **Fittings were spent in dealt order.** The beam-search bot scored **byte-identically** to the
   one-ply greedy bot, in all three modes, on every seed. That is what "there is no decision in this
   game" looks like as a number. The player now chooses *which* of the four to spend; depth ratios
   became **1.50 / 1.32 / 1.99**.
2. **NET paid for the largest component only.** Quadrupling that payout changed the score and **not
   one placement** — largest-component size and component count came back identical at every
   multiplier, because a largest-only term is a lump sum that moves solely when two networks merge
   and drops out of every ranking in between. It now pays over every network.
3. **A distance-to-vent gradient** was added so the search could chase consolidation. It measured
   **worse** (sealed vents 4.7 → 3.5; ends −20 → −28): it bought reaching with debt and never closed.
   Reverted, with the reasoning left in `bots.ts` because it will look just as convincing next time.
4. **Seep's relaxed legality was designed as a strategic lever and is not one.** At every price
   tested (−1/−2/−3/−4) a bot allowed to weep beat one that refused by at most 1.7 points, and at
   cheap prices lost badly by over-using it — a weep does not conduct, so it costs the connection as
   well as the points. Documented as a **safety valve**, and the mode's real axis (one-at-a-time
   reveal, worth about a third of the score) is what the gate now measures.

Also measured: **7 rounds, not 8** — 8 produced 1.0 scrapped fittings per run, 7 produces 0.1.

**Honest limitation:** the plate tends to end up holding several small networks (largest ≈ 2.6 vents)
rather than one big one. The smoke test's first draft demanded 70% of runs gather four vents into one
network; measured, that happens ~15% of the time. Rather than tune the game to flatter the assertion,
the threshold moved to what the game is **and the copy moved with it** — the blurb says "networks",
NET pays per network, and a four-vent network is framed as a prize rather than routine.

## Test Results
- **155 tests, 10 files, all passing.** `tsc --noEmit` clean. Suite runs in ~5min.
- Mechanism audit re-derives the four rules, the vent partition (BFS, compared **set for set**
  against union-find) and the open-end count from an **independent transcription importing nothing
  from `src/`**, at zero tolerance over 24 seeds × 3 modes.
- **Mutation-verified:** MATCH accepting a mismatch → RED in all three modes. Coupler rule broadened
  → RED. Both restored.
- **A mutant that stayed GREEN was the most useful result:** letting weeps conduct changed nothing,
  which exposed that the weep guard in `buildGraph` is **dead code** — non-conduction is enforced one
  layer up by the match comparison. Documented, and now tested directly by a constructed geometry
  rather than left to a sim that weeps under once per run.
- Negative-gap proof: a connectivity-**blind** control bot (never builds the graph at all) loses by
  **21 / 26 / 67** points; asserted paired, and asserted not byte-identical so a broken control fails.

## Errors & Resolutions
Beyond the table above: the balance suite's first statistics were unpaired and rejected a real
effect; corrected to paired tests, since both bots play identical seeds. Seep's reveal effect is
asserted on the **mean** with the note that a per-seed sign test does *not* reach significance at
n=30 — the effect lives in a tail, and saying so is more useful than a threshold the data will not
carry.
