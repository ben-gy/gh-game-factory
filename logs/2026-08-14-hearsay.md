## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Hearsay / ben-gy/hearsay
- **Genre:** card
- **Core loop:** hold one piece of testimony, draw one, play one; its power resolves publicly. Last
  one standing, or the strongest testimony when the docket runs dry.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, host-authoritative with genuinely hidden hands, 2–6
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM
- **Engine modules:** net, turn, rematch, lobby (+ qr), rng, sound, storage, mobile

## The one deviation from the shape, and why
Five of sixteen cards are the **Bailiff**: name a rival and a rank, and they are out if you are right.
In the original a miss is a wasted turn, which makes a third of the deck a lottery ticket. Here a miss
still pays — the caller, **and only the caller**, is told whether the testimony is higher or lower.

Three independent judges in the design fan-out named this the best idea across four candidate designs.
Two of them then found its flaw: announcing the hint to the **table** makes the read a public good, so
the caller spends the whole turn and every rival gets it free — the card then differentiates nobody
and gets worse the more players there are. Shipped private, with each rival's current read rendered on
their row from the same belief vector the bot decides on, because a read you cannot see is a read you
cannot use.

## The sim overruled the design twice
1. **A flat standing target** ran 4P Inquiry to **13.9 hearings**. Scaling the post with the table
   brought every mode at every seat count into 3–9.
2. Acting first is worth **54.5–58.3 %** of a single hearing at two seats. The obvious fix — give the
   opening to whoever is behind — made Full Brief 2P **worse**, 49.8/50.2 → **41.4/58.6**: a player
   only opens *while behind*, so whoever wins the tied rounds banks a lead in exactly the rounds the
   rule does not touch, and opens more often overall. Reverted. The real cause was a **sampling
   artifact of short matches** (a 3P match runs 4.3 hearings, and an unoffset rotation hands hearing 1
   to the same index every time), fixed by offsetting the rotation once per match.
   - A first attempt at that offset used `floor(seed / 7) % n`, which **correlated with the
     simulator's own seed stride** and manufactured a nine-point four-player bias that did not exist.
     A weak hash there does not make the game unfair; it makes the measurement lie.

## The draw pile survives the host leaving
The fleet's previous hidden-info game (squirm) recovered only because it had **no pile** — it dealt
the whole deck into hands — and its note says a future game with a face-down pile must "design it out,
or accept the round ends there". This does neither, because the pile is a **remainder**:

```
pile + set-aside  ==  composition − discards − face-up burns − attested hands
```

Every term on the right is public or attestable by its owner. Only the **order** is lost, and the
order was never known to anybody, so it is re-shuffled — unobservable by construction. Proven
card-for-card across five seeds.

## Errors & Resolutions
Three defects found by gates rather than by playing. Two were mine.

1. **The public snapshot carried the deal seed.** The deal is `deal(seed, mode, roster)`, and mode and
   roster are public — so any guest could re-run it in a console and read every hand and the entire
   deck order, with no hand, no rank and no event ever appearing in a snapshot, and the leak test green
   throughout, because it looked for the *secrets* and not for the thing that *generates* them. Found
   by an adversarial netcode review. `seed` removed from `PublicState`, `seed` now first on the
   forbidden-key list, mutation-verified red.
2. **A two-player hearing whose host left was a walkover that never happened.** The promoted peer
   rebuilds with the departed seat already dead, so forfeiting it is a no-op and `advance()` — which
   normally notices a collapse — is never reached, leaving the survivor on a live board with nobody to
   play and no way for it to end. Found in the two-tab smoke test; no unit test saw it because every
   unit test forfeited a seat that was still alive. Fixed with an explicit collapse check, plus an
   immediate takeover when nobody is left to attest (it otherwise sat out a 4s deadline).
   Mutation-verified red.
3. **Control borders were drawn with the hairline colour** at 1.74–2.11:1, against a fill only 1.21:1
   above the page — so the border carried nearly the whole "this is tappable" signal, below the 3:1
   floor WCAG sets for exactly that. Invisible to the palette contrast test, which proves the palette's
   colours are legible and *not which one got applied where*. Found by sampling computed styles on the
   rendered page. Now a source-level guard, which immediately caught one more (`.seg`).

Also fixed from the browser pass: the private hint was computed but never shown to the player (the
signature mechanic was invisible); "the The Verdict" everywhere an article was composed; "You names
the Clerk" in the log; a self-targeted Retrial reading "Vetch sends Vetch back"; a dead vertical gap
between the log and the hand; and a docket row sliced by the scroll boundary in landscape.
