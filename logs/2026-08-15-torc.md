## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Torc / ben-gy/torc
- **Genre:** board
- **Core loop:** Ride a torc in a straight line; it leaves a stud where it was, and a jump turns over
  every stud it crossed — including your own. A line of four (five on the largest field) comes off
  the board and scores, and costs you one of your torcs, which you choose.
- **Multiplayer:** live P2P, **lockstep**, 2 players. No authoritative simulation — both peers step
  identical state from an exchanged ply log. Every message carries the WHOLE log.
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM points over an SVG lattice + a Canvas 2D particle layer
- **Engine modules used:** net, rematch, turn, lobby (+QR), rng, sound, storage, mobile, identity
  (pinned at `v1.4.0`, verified installed)

## Juice
Procedural metal cues (`set`, `ride`, `flip`, `lift`, `give`); studs turn over with a 3D flip pitched
up along the run; a spark streak along the ride; particle bursts and a screen shake scaled to how
many studs turned; haptics on flips and claims; a 3-2-1 count-in with audio. All degrade under
`prefers-reduced-motion`. A 200-particle cap was added after driving a whole game through in twenty
seconds buried the field in dots.

## What the simulation actually changed (principle #18)
The sim was built and baselined BEFORE the modes were fixed, and it overruled the obvious design.

1. **The middle field's line length.** 55 points with a line of FIVE — the source's own shape — clogs:
   18–32% of strong-vs-strong games end with nobody able to ride, 13% are outright draws, because
   studs accumulate faster than claims clear them. **Dropping that field to a line of four took jams
   and draws to 0%** with a 7% blowout rate. Two confident fixes were tried first and both failed:
   fewer torcs barely moved it (13% jams), a lower target swapped the jam for a 39% blowout rate.
2. **The failure is invisible to a cheap search.** Measured at the budget the fast test uses: **0%
   jams**. At the deeper budget: **32%**. The clog is *caused by good play* — strong sides block each
   other's lines, so claims are rarer and studs pile up. Found by mutation-testing the ratchet: the
   run length was reverted and the test **stayed green**, so the termination bounds were moved to the
   deeper budget, where reverting it now goes red (25% jam vs a 0.10 bound).
3. **A swap/pie rule was designed and then dropped.** At n=40 the first-mover rate read 35% on one
   field and 63% on another, which looks like a real bias worth a permanent rule. At n=120 the three
   fields read **50%, 52% and 46% (±9)**. No bias; no rule.
4. **First-scorer win rate: 56% / 67% / 65% at n=160.** This is the metric that tests the design's own
   thesis — scoring costs you a torc, so a lead should be worth something without deciding the game.
   At n=15 the same measurement read 93/75/75%, pooling to 81%, which would have bought a redesign of
   the small field for nothing.
5. **Search width 8 is DEEPER than no narrowing at all** (depth 6 vs 3 on the biggest field at 400ms),
   so the top tier's depth cap was raised above what it can reach and the time budget made the binding
   constraint.

  `https://benrichardson.dev`). This is the documented fail-open path and the two-tab mesh formed
  anyway — TURN is an upgrade, never a dependency. Re-checked on production.
- The design panel's judges wanted a rule grafted in ("the Forfeit" — the torc you give up is the one
  you just rode). **Declined and logged to EXPANSION_IDEAS.md instead**: it is undefined for the case
  where the OPPONENT is forced to claim on your turn (a first-class case here), and adopting it after
  the shape had been balance-validated would have meant shipping an unmeasured ruleset.
