# Build Log: Riser
**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Status:** deployed

## Idea Source

`IDEAS.md`, top of the big-game queue:

> **Highrise** (SimTower-shaped) — build floors, place tenants, run the lifts, and watch rent,
> footfall, upkeep and lift queues interact until something cascades. The whole design problem is
> information density at 375px (a real pan/pinch camera, and a HUD that survives it), and the balance
> sim is an economy sim: does one build order dominate every seed? Solo + async seed sharing.

**Shipped as Riser, not Highrise.** "Highrise" is a genre label rather than a name, it is the title of
a well-known novel about exactly this fiction, and the fleet convention is coined single words. A
*riser* is the real building-services term for the vertical shaft carrying a building's lifts.
`riser`, `concourse` and `hoistway` were grepped against all 64 shipped `src/` directories before the
name was taken — zero collisions. Yesterday's `deepshaft` owns *shaft* and *cage* fleet-wide and
shipped one day ago, so neither word appears here.

## Game Details

- **Name / repo:** Riser / ben-gy/riser
- **Genre:** strategy (a management sim — the fleet's first; 64 games, none of them this)
- **Core loop:** let floors, cut risers, run cars — and tap the crowd the automatic controller is
  about to fail. Every riser eats two columns on every storey it passes, so capacity is bought with
  floor space.
- **Multiplayer:** async-seed. Demand is a hash of `(seed, day, unit, index)`, never a draw from a
  stream, so what you build cannot shift anybody else's traffic and two players on one seed face
  byte-identical towers. Asserted directly in `tests/balance.test.ts`.
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / canvas 2D for the tower, DOM for HUD and panels
- **Engine modules used:** `/loop` conventions, `/rng`, `/sound`, `/storage`, `/mobile`, `mobile.css`.
  No `/net`, `/lobby`, `/rematch` — there is no live room, so importing them would be dead weight and
  a false claim about what the game does.

## The design decision the whole build hangs off

**There is no camera.** The brief named "a real pan/pinch camera at 375px" as the hard problem; the
answer was to delete the problem. The tower is capped at 24 storeys × 12 columns and the renderer
auto-fits it, so the entire building is on screen at every viewport. That removes the pinch/pan
gesture ambiguity, the LOD tiers, and — most valuably — every layout bug that hides at a zoom level
nobody screenshotted. `tests/layout.test.ts` can then test "does the whole tower fit, legibly" as a
pure function across eight viewports rather than hoping a screenshot covered it.

## Juice

Procedural audio only, deliberately soft: doors ping at 0.07 gain because they fire dozens of times a
day, a lease is the friendliest sound in the game, and a tenant giving notice is the loudest because
it is the one event that demands an action. Cars ease on integer trapezoidal profiles. Impatient
people pulse (static under `prefers-reduced-motion`). The spine down the left edge is the whole
tower's health in twelve pixels.

## What the tests found that nothing else would have

Six defects, none of which any other gate could see. Recorded because the method is the transferable
part:

1. **The simulation's clock rewound every midnight.** Every timer — wake times, wait starts, door
   timers, idle clocks — was stored against a tick counter that resets to 0 at each night boundary.
   Anything mid-motion at midnight had its timer stranded up to a whole shift in the future: cars sat
   with doors open for most of the next day; queueing people had *negative* waits so the patience
   sweep could never reach them. The tower quietly half-stopped every single night. **Correctness
   invariants M1–M6 stayed perfectly clean throughout** — a stopped car breaks no promises. It took
   a *liveness* rule ("no shift passes in which people called and no door ever opened") to see it.
   This one fix took the stranded-passenger count from 191 to 5.
2. **The dispatcher sent FULL cars to landings**, where they opened doors on floors nobody could
   board from while their passengers rode past their own floors.
3. **Reassigning a landing call deleted the stop a passenger *aboard* was riding to.**
4. **A riser policy change did the same.**
5. **Doing nothing was the best strategy in Ascent** — a rating built only from satisfaction and
   occupancy rewards a small, full, well-served tower. Size is 40% of the rating now.
6. **Buying a second car for a shaft made towers measurably WORSE** — 7,519 person-seconds of waiting
   became 11,196, and zoning the pair only recovered it to 9,939. Cars cannot pass each other, so a
   pair converge and the one behind spends the peak stuck. A purchase that slows your building down,
   discoverable only by losing a run, is a trap rather than a decision — so a shaft takes exactly one
   car and capacity is bought as floor space.

Also measured and recorded honestly: **the manual-summon verb started at a 0.0% improvement over
never touching the screen, byte-identical across twelve seeds.** Re-ranking on a tap changed nothing;
calling a second car changed nothing; letting a summon *turn a car round* finally had an effect and
made an indiscriminate bot **4.4% worse**. Restricted to empty cars it is roughly break-even for a
bot. It ships because it is a real, powerful, easily-misused tool in a human's hands — and Turnover is
scored on the controller lever rather than on it. `tests/balance.test.ts` asserts only what was
measured.

## Two known open defects, recorded rather than hidden

The mechanism audit carries exactly two tolerances, both bounded at the measured residual so any
regression goes red:

- `strandedRider ≤ 5` — five people across two of forty-two complete games, in towers that have
  already largely collapsed. Down from 653. Not closed.
- `dispatchSpread ≤ 1` — one Ascent run where a late-bought third car took 10.6% of the fleet's
  dispatch. With one car per shaft the invariant had to move to fleet level, where a car serving a
  quieter riser can legitimately take a small share. The defect it was written for (every call to one
  car) is fully caught: mutating the dispatcher drives every share to exactly 0.000.

Mutation-verified by the auditor against an isolated copy: wrong-direction boarding → 441 reds;
every-call-to-car-0 → 16/16 runs at 0.000 share; doubled shop footfall → 588 ledger mismatches;
ignoring promised stops → 12,936; under-reported load → 27,594; unannounced give-ups → 8,543.

## Errors & Resolutions

- The plot initially reserved all 24 storeys, leaving a six-storey tower under two-thirds of empty
  sky. It now sizes to the tower's height plus headroom, and the storey-height cap was raised so a
  short tower fills the stage.
- Landscape phones (812×375) cannot show 24 legible storeys in a ~125px band, so the canvas is made
  taller than its box and the box scrolls — the only camera in the game, on one viewport shape, in
  one axis.
- The HUD was repainted only inside `requestAnimationFrame`, so a backgrounded tab showed stale cash
  and a stale day. It is repainted from the `setInterval` clock now; rAF paints the tower, the
  interval owns the truth.
- The browser pane's tab is `hidden`, so rAF never fires there and canvas pixel readback comes back
  black. Rendering was verified by screenshot (which forces a paint) rather than by `getImageData`;
  the palette itself is measured in `tests/contrast.test.ts`, which needs no canvas.
