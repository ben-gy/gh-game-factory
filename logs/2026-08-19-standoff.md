# Build Log: Standoff
**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Status:** deployed

## Idea Source
IDEAS.md, first entry of the big-game queue, taken and removed:

> **Standoff** (Frozen Synapse-shaped) — both sides issue orders for the same five
> seconds, both resolve simultaneously, and you watch the replay together. Sight lines,
> cover, and guessing what they guessed. The ideal P2P shape: orders are a few bytes,
> latency is irrelevant, and the replay is a shareable link. vs 2, simultaneous-turn; a
> real opponent bot for solo.

Design was run as an ultracode workflow: a prior-art sweep, three independent designs
(different emphases: genre fidelity / phone legibility / systems depth), a three-judge
panel, a synthesised spec and five adversarial critiques. 13 agents, all Opus 5.

**Two designers independently proposed renaming the game "Enfilade".** Kept as
**Standoff**: two of three judges said so explicitly (the slug was already scaffolded,
it greps clean fleet-wide, and "enfilade" costs the player a word they do not know).

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Standoff / ben-gy/standoff
- **Genre:** strategy (simultaneous-turn tactics)
- **Core loop:** Draw each unit a route and a direction to watch; both sides commit the
  same five seconds blind; both plans resolve at once and you watch the film. A shot
  kills, a unit only fires inside its watch wedge, and the mast in the middle is what
  actually wins.
- **Multiplayer:** async-seed (share the identical board). **Live P2P was cut — see below.**
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / Canvas 2D board + DOM screens
- **Engine modules used:** mobile (hardenViewport + mobile.css), sound, storage, rng

## What the design workflow changed before a line was written
- **Prior art:** windup (2026-07-17) already owns blind-commit-then-watch-the-reveal and
  frostward (2026-07-18) self-describes as simultaneous-commit, so the registry entry
  claims neither. The honest firsts are: simultaneous-turn TACTICS with sight lines and
  cover, continuous-time order-window resolution, and a bot that solves a
  SIMULTANEOUS-MOVE game to a mixed policy rather than searching a move tree.
- **Vocabulary:** the scaffold's mode ids `skirmish` (taken 6x) and `blackout` (nightwire)
  were caught by a judge reading the in-flight source and renamed to
  `crossfire` / `ambush` / `roving`. "Cone" was renamed to "wedge" (frostward's Gale arc
  owns the sweeping-arc identity) and the objective meter is `hold`, not `claim` (75 files).

## Juice
Procedural audio only (11 cues, oscillator envelopes): select, waypoint, stance, commit,
fire, kill, hold, win, lose. Tracers linger four ticks so a shot is never a single missed
frame. Threat wedges shade and outline the ground each enemy covers, live under a moving
thumb. `prefers-reduced-motion` fast-forwards the replay rather than animating it.

## Test Results
- **196 tests, 7 files, all passing.** rules (33) · balance (24) · mechanism (16) ·
  contrast (28) · tutorial (10) · manifest (~40) · source-hygiene (~45).
- **Mechanism audit, zero tolerance,** re-deriving every rule by a DIFFERENT method than
  the game uses: line of sight as a supercover grid walk (game: precomputed bitset), the
  wedge quantised via `Math.atan2` (game: sixteen integer dot products), range by square
  root. Imports no geometry from `src/rules.ts`. Counters for shots through walls, out of
  range, outside the wedge, at the dead and by the dead all come out at exactly 0 over
  thousands of shots.
- **Mutation-verified twice.** (a) Six violations planted by hand — the auditor reports
  every one. (b) Applying deaths immediately instead of at end-of-tick (destroying
  simultaneity) turns the mirror invariant RED; restoring it turns it green.
- **The mirror invariant is the real fairness gate:** rotate the position and both order
  sets, and the outcome must be exactly the rotated outcome. Zero violations over
  hundreds of positions in all three modes.

## What the balance sim caught — none of it visible any other way
1. **Roving measured 0% against 83%.** The mast alternated halves "for an equal number of
   turns each", which is only fair in a match that reaches the turn cap — and matches ended
   on turn three, inside the first away phase. It is a simultaneous rotational PAIR now.
2. **Generation fell back on 100% of roving seeds.** Five mast fields had to be kept clear,
   forcing every board above its own lane band, so every map was the least-bad reject:
   wide open, fights decided on contact, matches over in 1.6 turns. Only counting how often
   the generator gave up found this; that count is now asserted at zero.
3. **A third of all matches ended with nobody alive, scored a draw.** Trades are deliberate
   here, so mutual annihilation is not an edge case. The mast breaks the tie now.
4. **The bot took every trade offered,** because a trade is exactly neutral when both sides'
   bodies are worth the same. Its own units are worth more to it now.
5. **The search won 57-43 from one seat** on maps that are mirror-identical with a resolver
   that is mirror-exact — its shortlist ties broke by tile index, which does not survive
   rotating the board. Found by a random-vs-random control arm: the bias vanished without
   the search, which is what proved it was not a rules bug.
6. **A Marksman could shoot into the enemy spawn on tick 0** (range 13 against a 13-row
   spawn gap; 70% of matches had a kill in the opening window). Range is 10.
7. **Over half the board was solid wall** at 4x4 blocks — walls read as the background with
   the floor as the shapes. Capped at 3x3; a test asserts open ground dominates.

## TWO FINDINGS RECORDED AS MEASURED RATHER THAN FIXED
Both are in `tests/balance.test.ts` with the measurement rather than a flattering bound,
and both are stated in CONTRIBUTING.md:

- **P(the side ahead after turn 2 wins) sits near 0.8.** Principle #18 wants this near
  chance early and spiking late. With one-shot kills and three units the first body really
  is most of the match. The genre's answer is units that survive more than one hit — a
  change to the resolver, the tuning and every number in the balance file at once, which
  could not be made and re-verified in this pass. **This is the game's main open weakness.**
- **Roving retains a residual seat preference in bot-vs-bot play.** The GAME is exact (the
  mirror invariant proves it at zero tolerance); the search still plays that mode slightly
  better from one seat. Jittering shortlist ties removed most of it, not all. Crossfire and
  Ambush are fair across three independent seed families.

## LIVE P2P WAS CUT — stated plainly
The queued idea asks for vs-2 simultaneous turns, and the architecture is built for it: a
turn is a pure function of (seed, mode, both order blobs), an order is a dozen bytes, and
nothing about the board or the outcome would need to cross the wire. It was cut because the
full contract this factory requires — commit/reveal, host transfer, late join, rematch, and
the six-step two-peer smoke test — could not be built AND verified end to end in the same
pass as the game. Shipping unverified netcode is worse than shipping none. The P2P-only
tests written early in the run were deleted rather than left testing unused code, and the
README says the same thing. **This is a scope cut, not a completed requirement.**

## Errors & Resolutions
- The contrast gate found six failures including **blue vs amber at 1.07:1**. Investigating
  showed the requirement is UNSATISFIABLE: both sides must clear 3:1 against a dark floor,
  which puts both luminances above ~0.17, and two colours above 0.17 cannot be 3:1 apart.
  That is a finding about the design, not a threshold to lower — those meanings are carried
  by shape instead (an X for a dead unit, a bar for a rail, a ring for selection, a glyph
  per unit type) and the tests assert those channels exist. The rail WAS a real failure at
  2.07:1 and was lightened.
- The wedge was originally a dot-product-against-cosine test, which is not exactly
  symmetric: the integer direction vectors differ in length in the fourth decimal, so a
  target on the exact edge was admitted on one side and refused on the other. It is a
  sector-index comparison now — exactly symmetric, and it agrees with the picture the
  player is looking at.
- Thinning the walls reintroduced a spawn-sightline violation on one seed in forty, because
  the repair could not find an unprotected tile on the line and the least-bad fallback
  shipped it anyway. Candidates that violate it are now INELIGIBLE for the fallback.
