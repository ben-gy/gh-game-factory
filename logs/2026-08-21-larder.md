# Build Log: Larder
**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Status:** deployed

## Idea Source

`IDEAS.md`, top of the BIG-GAME queue, verbatim:

> **Wintering** (colony sim) — settlers with needs, weather, and a supply chain that can fail in ways
> nobody wrote down: a blocked path starves a wing, an illness takes the only cook. Solo, deep, with a
> run summary worth reading and a shared daily seed to compare against. Pause-and-issue-orders rather
> than twitch, which is also what makes it work on a phone.

**Renamed to Larder** on the prior-art agent's verdict. "Wintering" would have been the third
winter-titled game after `frostward` and `lastlight`, and `lastlight` is specifically a cold-survival
settlement game — on the catalogue page a browser reads it as that game's sequel. No name in the
68-game fleet is an `-ing` gerund either. "Larder" names the thing that fails rather than the weather.

## Game Details
- **Name / repo:** Larder / ben-gy/larder
- **Genre:** strategy
- **Core loop:** designate work on the board and rank six job classes; colonists take the
  highest-ranked job they can *reach*, and the snow keeps editing what is reachable.
- **Multiplayer:** none — solo plus a shared-seed link. Justified in the plan rather than skipped:
  the authoritative state is 143 tiles plus agents plus a job queue plus a ledger, and two people
  re-ranking one work board is an argument inside a settings menu, not a game.
- **Stack / render:** vanilla-ts / DOM grid (143 cells, no camera, no scrolling)
- **Engine modules used:** rng, sound, storage, mobile + mobile.css. **Not** net/lobby/rematch/turn,
  and `source-hygiene.test.ts` asserts they are never imported, so the no-backend claim is structural.
  Exactly **one** package reaches the browser, confirmed from `dist/**/*.js.map`.

## What the multi-agent pass actually bought

Ran under ultracode, all subagents pinned to Opus 5.

- **Prior art (1 agent):** read all 67 registry entries and grepped every shipped `src/`. Verdict

- **Design fan-out (3 agents, 3 angles) + judge panel (3 lenses):** all three judges picked a

- **The synthesis agent died mid-response** on an oversized context (~540KB of designs + judgments) and

  one phase that did not work as designed.
- **Adversarial critique (3 agents on the merged spec):** this is the pass that paid for the whole
  thing. All three independently **simulated the snow rule** and found the same thing — the design's
  central mechanic had its sign backwards.

## The findings, in the order they were found

**1. The snow rule was inverted (found by all three critics, independently).** The spec had drifts
growing from drifts. Measured: a 3-wide corridor severed in 90s and a 1-wide in 95s — a ratio of
**0.95×** against a claimed 4.5×. Widening the artery, the game's stated central skill, was pure cost
with a slightly *negative* benefit, and two mandatory gates were pre-committed to asserting it. The
cause is structural, not tuning: before anything reaches depth 2 the term is zero for every tile
whatever the width, and once it engages the wider corridor has *more* mutually-reinforcing neighbours.
Fixed by the physically true rule — **snow drifts against obstructions** — and the constants were then
set by a harness written before any other file, per principle #18. Measured after: **400 / 600 / 800
ticks** for widths 1/2/3 under storm, with a storm running at least 450 ticks. `400 < 450 < 800` is the
inequality the design rests on, and `tests/rules.test.ts` asserts the *property*, not the seconds.

**2. The hero HUD number was undefined for the first 30 seconds** and a coin flip thereafter: it was a
300-tick sample of a process with 0.72 expected events per window. Replaced with an exact analytic
form.

**3. Auto-pause was unbounded** — one snow batch severing a wing would have raised a banner per
affected job. Now: one per tick with reasons coalesced, a 150-tick cooldown, hysteresis on the stock
trigger, and a set so any one job raises at most one banner per run.

## What the balance sim found that nothing else could

Every one of these was invisible to unit tests *and* to playing it:

- an **infinite spill→haul→spill loop** — 800 spills in a 12-day run, from a job that could never be
  completed existing at all;
- a zero-initialised `moveTo` that **teleported the entire colony to tile 0 on the first tick** (the
  symptom was "The Crossing produces zero wood and nobody ever eats");
- a **doorway that drifted shut on every seed with no job to dig it out**, freezing the whole fleet
  with a full larder — interaction C firing correctly with no counter-play;
- The Crossing's **neck was a sealed pocket** (three rock rows with staggered gaps), so no policy ever
  survived — a broken loop, not difficulty;
- Fastness's **ninth colonist spawned inside a wall**, unreachable, on every seed;
- a regression I introduced myself: after making the days-of-cover figure count raw food, the cook's
  stop-condition inherited it and **nobody ever cooked** — meals produced fell 83 → 8 in one run.

## What the mechanism audit found

`tests/helpers/audit.ts` keeps its own ledger and flood-fill and imports no arithmetic from the game.
It found the simulation **silently destroying resources**: crafting took its wood straight out of a
store and building zeroed the carried timber, both with no transaction at all, and a harvest reported
picking up less than it actually lifted. None of it was visible on screen, because the numbers the HUD
reads come from the containers, which were correct. Fixed with an explicit `Consume` event.

Six invariants, all at zero tolerance across 36 full winters (3 modes × 3 policies × 4 seeds, ~2s):
M1 conservation, M3 no teleported meal, M4 no impossible step, M5 no decision that sees reachable work
and takes none, M6 warmth is exactly a flood-fill room, is never the outdoors, and is paid for in
timber. Each is **mutation-verified** by a planted violation of its own kind.

## What the browser pass found that no test could

- **`sfx.unlock()` threw and it was the first statement in a click handler**, so *every* top-bar and
  tab-bar button silently did nothing while looking perfectly normal. The board still worked, because
  it plays its sound *after* it acts. This would have shipped an unplayable game.
- The **tutorial could never advance past step 1**: its completion was checked only inside a tick, and
  step 1 is paused. Painting was also gated on rAF, which never fires in a hidden tab.
- `--cell` was declared on `:root` and `--chrome` overridden on `body`, so **the override was computed
  and then completely ignored** — the tutorial card sat on top of the tab bar its own last step tells
  you to open.
- The `≥900px` branch reserved a 360px side rail **that the code never created**.
- The sheet's repaint was deleting the **modal's** backdrop, so tap-outside-to-dismiss silently did not
  exist on the help and about panels.
- The chrome budget was **8px short** (six grid gaps, not four) — invisible at 375×812, and it put the
  tab bar below the fold at 375×635.

## Juice

Procedural audio on fifteen declared cues (all guarded — audio can never break the interface again),
colonists tween across tiles at their real move duration so deep snow visibly *labours*, auto-pause
with a coalesced banner, warm rooms ringed in amber, snow in three depths plus a hatch on impassable.
No screen shake: this is a quiet game and shake would be a lie about its register.

## Measured balance (30 seeds, mean souls saved)

| | router | steady | blind | waller |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| longwinter | **2.40** | 1.67 | 1.37 | 0.60 |
| fastness | **2.10** | 0.50 | 0.70 | 0.00 |
| crossing | 1.50 | **1.53** | 0.00 | 0.00 |

`blind` (never clears snow) survives 40% of seeds at home, 10% in Fastness and **0%** in The Crossing —
how much the verb matters differs per mode, which is the honest form of "three different games".
`waller` is never best, so the reciprocal fuel curve does punish walling the map in.

**Stated honestly:** the plan hoped for a 15pp router-vs-steady gap and an argmax that differs by mode.
Neither survived measurement. The router-vs-steady margin is real but modest, and The Crossing's is
inside the noise, so it is **not asserted there** — on a map with one neck, *whether* you dig matters
enormously and *when* barely registers. Sample size moved these numbers every time it grew.
